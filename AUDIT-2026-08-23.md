**Scope.** Four questions asked of the whole tree: *what is broken, what costs
more than it should, what behaves in a way that will surprise its own author,
and what is missing.* Every Rust crate (15, ~94k lines), the Tauri shell
(~6.8k lines), all 30 TypeScript window modules (~21.5k lines), the stylesheet,
and the tools under `tools/`.

**Baseline.** `main` at `2c9e8ce`, working tree clean at audit start.
`cargo test --workspace --all-targets`: green. `cargo clippy --workspace
--all-targets`: zero warnings. `npm --prefix app run test`: 537 passed, 0
failed.

**Method, and its limits.** Source read first: every crate was read end to end,
with candidate defects traced through callers before being recorded. The
highest-severity findings were then **re-verified by hand against the source a
second time**, and the browser build was exercised over the dev fixtures (the
`dev-fixtures` example plus Vite) to confirm the fixture/wire claims. No
long-run profiling was done; performance findings are readings of code paths
with their own published measurements where available. Each finding below says
whether it was **verified** (read directly, twice) or **located** (found by
systematic read, mechanism traced, not independently re-read). Line numbers are
against `2c9e8ce`.

**Relationship to [`AUDIT.md`](AUDIT.md) and
[`AUDIT-CORRECTNESS.md`](AUDIT-CORRECTNESS.md)** (both 18 August). Those audits

## Major findings

### M1 · A torn append permanently poisons the semantic lane's vector store
**bug · verified · `crates/girsa-lane/src/vectors.rs:758–808, 813–839`**

`read()` detects a torn/corrupt tail and records `self.torn`. Nothing ever
truncates those bytes: `append()` opens with `.append(true)` and writes at
end-of-file, *after* the garbage. On the next open, `read()` stops at the old
torn byte again — so every vector written after the crash, validly and at real
embedding cost, sits invisible forever: `has()` misses them (the resume job
re-embeds them, duplicating days of CPU), `nearest()` never sees them, and the
file grows each session. Only `restarted()` — explicit destruction — recovers.
This contradicts the module's own contract, *"the only cost of stopping is the
batch it was on"* (`job.rs:10–13`). Fix is small: truncate to the last good
offset before appending.

### M2 · One invalid UTF-8 byte makes an entire personal layer read as empty — and compaction then destroys it
**bug · verified · `crates/girsa-personal/src/log.rs:317–325`; `store.rs:133`**

```rust
let Ok(body) = std::fs::read_to_string(&self.path) else {
    return Live { records: Vec::new(), … trouble: Vec::new() };
};
```

`read_to_string` fails on any invalid UTF-8 *anywhere* in the file, plus any
permission/lock error. All of these are indistinguishable from "file does not
exist yet": the layer reads as empty with **no trouble line**, though the
module's contract promises a torn last line "reports and skips". A power-loss
tear that splits a two-byte Hebrew character is enough. From there the damage
compounds: the next automatic compaction rewrites the file from the (empty)
index — permanent, unrecoverable loss of every correction, mark, folder, note
and document in that layer. Related instance of the same shape: a *missing*
log is surfaced nowhere else either — see also M3's write-side twin.

### M3 · A crash mid-import can silently revert ID assignment to line position — the exact defect §1 exists to prevent
**bug · verified · `crates/girsa-corpus/src/import/mod.rs:988–1021, 239–247`**

`import::write` emits multi-megabyte `segments.jsonl` with truncating
`fs::write` — no write-beside-and-rename. A crash mid-write tears the file;
next import, `Previous::on_the_shelf` → `read_back` fails → `Self::none()` →
"the ordinals come out of enumeration position exactly as they always did."
That is T1: every correction, mark, link and Ksav citation anchored to the old
names now points at shifted text, and the only hint is one stderr line saying
"first import". The repo knows the correct pattern twice over (`fetch.rs`
writes `.part` + rename; `beside.rs` argues the doctrine in detail) — the two
writers that matter most never got the memo. Same shape: `catalogue`/
`uncatalogue` truncate `personal/works/index.jsonl`.

### M4 · Proximity search does not match what its contract promises for three or more words
**bug · verified (implementation); tantivy semantics checked against pinned source ·
`crates/girsa-search/src/index.rs:1594–1610`; contract at `torat_emet.rs:64–67`**

`Near{N}` is implemented as one `PhraseQuery` per word-ordering with
`set_slop(gap)`. Tantivy's slop budget is a **sum across consecutive pairs**,
not per pair. So `Near{2}` over `א ב ג` against text where the words sit two
apart each — precisely "at most 2 words between them, in any order" — costs
2+2=4 > 2 and is silently missed. No false positives in the other direction;
pure recall loss in the mode whose entire pitch is *asked for exactly*. Either
the doc changes or each pair needs its own constraint.

### M5 · Dilug and notarikon drop pagination and clone every matching segment whole
**bug + inefficiency · located · `crates/girsa-search/src/bar.rs:515–569` (via `by_instrument` :420–456)**

`over_the_text` receives `paging` and discards it; every hit carries the
segment's full text (the crate's own comments note a 1,275,307-char largest
segment) into an unbounded answer, while every other mode pages at
`PROBE_LIMIT`. A two-letter-name dilug at skip 2 over a large sefer returns
thousands of full-text rows in one response; the CLI reports `pages = 1` and
the memory spike rides along.

### M6 · Re-running the link importer leaves every downstream cache stale, with no signal
**bug · located · `crates/girsa-link/src/inbound.rs:68–70`, `touching.rs:186–204`,
`bin/girsa-link-import.rs:153–156`**

`girsa-link-import` rewrites shards and nothing else. After a re-import:
`inbound.built` still exists (so chains read the old graph's incoming half
against new shards), `touching.bits` fingerprints only segmentation (so masks
stay "Known" while counting old edges), and companions has no fingerprint at
all. A reader who re-imports after a Sefaria update and skips
`girsa-link-types` mixes two graphs everywhere, silently. The codebase invented
`NotThisSegmentation` and `Refused::incoming_unknown` for exactly this class;
the batch boundary skips it. (New instance, graph axis, of the invalidation
machinery the Aug-18 audit praised.)

### M7 · Chain traversal can silently miss edges whose ends sit at a different granularity
**bug · located · `crates/girsa-link/src/chain.rs:429–441` vs `lib.rs:208–216`,
`segment.rs:408–414`**

`Held::near` indexes point edge-ends by exact ordinal range `[at.from..=last]`,
but `far_end`'s overlap semantics treat a point anchor as covering descendants
(`Ordinal::covers` is prefix logic). After any cut/mint_between — the normal
post-re-segmentation state — a shard legitimately holds both `#7` and `#7.1`;
edges stored at the other granularity sort outside the slice and every
`trace`/`path`/`forks` hop through such a segment misses them. A shorter chain
that looks complete — the failure `Refused` exists to prevent. The equivalence
test sweeps only root ordinals, so it cannot catch this.

### M8 · `moved` — the scroll handler — holds the global state lock across whole-work disk reads
**bug/inefficiency · verified · `app/src-tauri/src/lib.rs:5720–5757`**

`let mut state = shared.lock()` … then `state.sefer(&leader_slug)?` and one
`state.sefer(slug)?` per follower while still holding the guard. `sefer` →
`load` → `shelf.read(slug)` is a tens-of-megabytes read on cache miss — easy to
reach with several panes and a 12-entry LRU. Every other command in the window
queues behind it. The file's own `hold()`/`held()` doctrine (lib.rs:544–583)
exists for exactly this and is applied to `open_sefer`, `copy`, `links`,
`send_to_ksav`… just not here. **Not among the nine commands the Aug-18 audit
named** — this is a tenth.

### M9 · Follower panes can be dragged back by a stale answer
**bug/race · verified · `app/src/main.ts:1023–1051`**

`whenMoved` fires per scroll position with no sequencing: two quick scrolls put
two IPC calls in flight and the older resolving later re-applies its moves —
followers, fraction-followers and scan page turns included — yanked back to the
stale position. `reported` dedupes identical positions only. The repo fixed
this exact shape in `search.ts` (Latest tickets) and `linksview`; this caller
never got one.

### M10 · A failing scan pane kills the draw loop — the bug this file already fixed once
**bug · verified · `app/src/main.ts:612–621`**

The sefer branch is protected by `readPane`'s resolve-not-reject shape, with a
comment (lines 595–600) telling the whole story of the black-rectangle
incident. The scan branch does `await api.scan(pane.slug)` bare: one rejection
(PDF moved, asset protocol trouble) propagates out of `draw()`'s loop and every
pane after it goes undrawn, plus an unhandled rejection. Same family: M28
below.

### M11 · `goToFraction` loads a window and never repaints it
**bug · located · `app/src/pane.ts:875–888`**

`render(at)` draws holes, `load(...)` fills `lines` — and nothing redraws.
Compare `goToId` (:943–946), which re-renders after its load. Scenario: leader
scrolls, unrelated pane follows by fraction into unloaded territory → a
permanently blank column with nothing to scroll.

### M12 · The lane column is the one panel without a stale-answer guard — and its answers take seconds
**bug/race · verified · `app/src/laneview.ts:68–83` via `search.ts:437`**

Seven panels take tickets; `LaneColumn.show` takes none, and its caller fires
it `void`-style after the literal results draw. Search A, then quickly search
B: A's slow model answer resolves last and draws **under B's literal hits**.
`latest.ts`'s own header names the lane as the motivating case. (Extends the
Aug-18 audit's finding 9, which caught the find bar but not this.)

### M13 · ScanView page metadata lands out of order; wrong-page rectangles get drawn and followers dragged back
**bug/race · located · `app/src/scanview.ts:332–348`**

Render is serialized; the three IPC fetches (`scanAt`/`scanWords`/`scanMarks`)
are not. Hold ArrowRight over a slow disk: page 6 renders, page 5's in-flight
answers land last → highlight rectangles from page 5 over page 6's image, and
`onMove(this.id, said.id)` reports the stale segment so following panes turn
backwards. Same class as M12.

### M14 · The OCR queue's jump-to-word highlights the wrong rectangle whenever the page has your highlights
**bug · verified · `app/src/scanview.ts:500` vs `:414–461`**

`drawMarks()` appends your highlight boxes **before** word boxes in every mode;
`correctWord` then indexes `this.overlay.children[index]` under the comment
*"correcting draws every word in order, so the nth box is the nth word."*
With any `yours` on the page, `children[index]` is an ink box: the reader is
shown — and scrolls to — the wrong rectangle, while the prefill text comes from
the right word. Saved index right, evidence wrong.

### M15 · Two rapid tab switches interleave rows into one list
**bug/race · located · `app/src/yoursview.ts:154–179`**

Click notes (slow read), then marks (fast): marks replace children and draw;
notes' rows then land underneath them under the marks tab. No ticket, no burn
on close.

### M16 · A second bookmark on the same place is silently swallowed
**bug · verified · `crates/girsa-note/src/mark.rs:205–222, 255–279, 493–524`**

A bookmark's identity hashes `(segment, 0, 0, "", "bookmark", "")` — label and
colour are applied *after* naming and are not part of the fingerprint anyway.
Every bookmark on one daf gets the same id; `add` sees "already", skips the
append, and returns the first mark. Adding "עיין בזה" after "להתחיל כאן"
succeeds invisibly and stores nothing. Same collision for two identical-span
highlights differing only in colour.

### M17 · A CRLF text file imports one-segment-per-line; the same content with LF imports per paragraph
**bug · verified · `crates/girsa-corpus/src/import/mine.rs:529–546`**

`text.split("\n\n")` cannot match `"\r\n\r\n"` — the `\r` sits between the
newlines — so a Windows-line-ended `.txt` takes the fallback branch: one
segment per line, different permanent IDs minted than the identical LF file,
contradicting the stated rule "a .txt is split where its author left blank
lines." For a library whose sources are Windows text files, this is not an
edge case.

### M18 · build-a-shelf's download and resume stories are both false
**bug/confusing · verified · `tools/build-a-shelf.mjs:160–191, 223, 255–288`**

Three separate defects: (a) `download()` fetches `content-length` for the
progress bar and never compares bytes seen against it, has no checksum, and
ignores stream errors — a dropped connection yields a truncated 1.28 GB zip
indistinguishable from complete; (b) resume is `existsSync(zip)`, so the
corrupt archive is cached forever and every retry dies in `unzip` with "unpack
it by hand"; (c) the header promises "every step checks whether its output is
already there and skips it," but steps 3–7 re-run unconditionally — an
interrupted step 5 re-does the multi-hour import and link import first. Also:
`built()`'s docstring says "a directory with anything in it" while the body is
bare `isDirectory()`, so an empty half-created dir counts as done forever; and
two steps are numbered 6.

### M19 · Export reports a landed correction as both applied and not-applied
**bug · verified · `crates/girsa-export/src/lib.rs:131–135`**

`anchored` counts patches by exact current segment id; `Open::corrected_by`
applies patches stored under old names via Standing resolution. After an
upstream split, a patch that lands in the exported words is absent from
`anchored`, so `stale += all − anchored` inflates: the header simultaneously
claims "הוחל תיקון אחד" and "תיקון אחד שלא חל" about the same patch, and a
genuinely-dead patch is counted in `stale` twice. Smaller sibling at :195–202:
`corrections == 0` with `noted > 0` prints "הוחלו 0 תיקונים".

### M20 · Saving a note runs a tantivy commit under the global state lock
**bug/inefficiency · located · `app/src-tauri/src/lib.rs:492–499, 6222–6223, 6343`**

`note_write`/`note_edit` call `state.searchable(&slug)` while holding the
guard; `searchable` → `make_searchable` → `bar.absorb` commits the index.
`make_searchable`'s own doc says the split exists precisely because absorbing
"has no business holding the state lock," and `add_mine` obeys. Every saved
keystroke-batch in a note stalls the whole window for a commit.

### M21 · Switching corpora freezes the window for the whole pile, including a model load
**inefficiency · located · `app/src-tauri/src/lib.rs:816–863`**

`choose_corpus` holds the state lock across the entire `open_corpus`: catalogue
read, index open, notes open, lexicon, and `Adjacency::open` — which loads the
neural model when the lane is on. Elsewhere model loading is treated as
thread-worthy; here it happens under the lock every command waits on.

## Inefficiencies

1. **OCR pages shipped over IPC as spread JS number arrays** —
   `[...new Uint8Array(...)]` materializes millions of boxed numbers per
   multi-MB PNG, serialized as JSON, during the exact job the pane promises
   won't block reading. `scanview.ts:705–709`.
2. **`paint()` walks every mark × a document-wide querySelector on every
   extend** — hundreds of scoped queries per frame-batch during edge scrolling
   at realistic mark counts. `pane.ts:554–596`.
3. **One luach IPC round trip per toolbar rebuild** — and the toolbar rebuilds
   on every draw; a local date would do, cached for the day.
   `main.ts:1362–1365`.
4. **Every single record appended creates and deletes a lock file** — around
   an append the lock's own header says is already line-safe; needed only
   against compaction renames. `personal/log.rs:495–518`.
5. **Compaction re-reads the whole layer to carry the tail** — a seek+stream
   gives identical semantics at O(tail) memory. `personal/log.rs:473–487`.
6. **Per-edge key allocation ×2 writers × 4.18M edges** in the link import hot
   loop. `link/store.rs:161–167`, `shards.rs:48–49`.
7. **MCP `Shelf::read` reparses a whole work per request, and `adjacent` does
   it per hit** — up to max_limit full-work parses in one call.
   `shelf.rs:369–379`, `nearby/adjacent.rs:319`.
8. **`seforim` normalizes the entire library's titles per call** — ~7,200
   allocations to look one thing up; the catalogue open is the obvious place
   for a normalized index. `mcp/tools.rs:1034–1046`.
9. **Find-inside recomputes the printed address per hit instead of per
   segment** — its sibling hoists exactly this. `inside.rs:209–214` vs :125–130.
10. **`dibur_span` re-tokenizes the whole drawn line once per candidate** —
    ~3× the dominant cost, per link row. `spans.rs:129–146`.
11. **`state()` ships the whole workspace plus the remembered place of every
    sefer ever opened, plus the key table, on every redraw.** `lib.rs:728–786`.
12. **Queue ranking re-sorts all ~28k waiting entries per call.**
    `fix/suspect.rs:569–577`.
